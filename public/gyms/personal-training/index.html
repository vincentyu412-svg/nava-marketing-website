Personal training business Selling hours, one at a time.

Personal training is the most personal sale in fitness. The price is high, the relationship is one-to-one, and every client takes up real hours on a real trainer's calendar. That changes what good marketing looks like: fewer, better leads, a fast reply, and a consultation that builds trust. Here is how PT studios usually work, and what we do about each part.

Trainer hours · this weekOpenConsultClient
Prime hours filled

High ticket means a consult, not a click

Personal training usually costs far more each month than a gym membership, so very few people buy it straight from an ad. The sale happens in a consultation, where a trainer listens to someone's goals, explains the plan and earns their trust. Everything before that point exists to book the consult.

Our ads and follow-up are built to put consultations on the calendar.

Capacity is counted in trainer hours

A trainer can only coach so many sessions a week, and the early morning and after-work slots go first. Growing a PT studio means filling open hours across the whole team, and sometimes offering semi-private sessions once the best hours are taken.

We point new clients toward the trainers and times with open hours.

Trust comes before the sale

People hiring a trainer are often nervous: about their fitness, an old injury or wasting money on something they won't stick with. Real trainers on camera, a plain explanation of how sessions work and a friendly first conversation do far more than any discount.

We plan creative that introduces your trainers as people, not stock photos.

Every lost lead is expensive

Because each client is worth a lot over a year, a missed call or a slow reply costs more than it would at a low-price gym. A consult that doesn't show hurts twice: the lost sale and an empty hour on a trainer's calendar that could have gone to someone else.

Follow-up within minutes, and reminders before every consultation.

Personal training marketing system Built for the consult.

Every GymPropel gym gets the same connected system. For a personal training studio, every part points at one moment: a consultation with the right trainer. Ads, follow-up, the website and the consult itself run as one, Vincent, the founder, is your strategist, and we report in clients who sign and stay rather than in leads.

  1. Gym Facebook ads

    Ads for people who want a coach

    Facebook and Instagram ads shown within driving distance, featuring your trainers and the kind of coaching you offer. The call to action is a free consultation, never a cut-price session.

  2. Google Ads for gyms

    Found for "personal trainer near me"

    People searching for a personal trainer nearby have already decided they want help. Search ads put your studio in front of them, with calls and consult bookings tracked. If enough locals search for a trainer in your area, we add search ads; if not, we don't.

  3. Gym website design

    A page that books the consult

    A fast mobile page that introduces your trainers, explains how sessions and packages work and makes booking a consultation easy. It is usually yours to keep.

  4. Gym lead follow-up

    Booked before they call someone else

    Every enquiry gets a text, an email and a voicemail within minutes, offering consult times that fit your trainers' calendars. Anyone who goes quiet keeps hearing from you for weeks.

  5. Gym membership sign-ups

    A consult that ends in a package

    Reminders so consults show up, a clear structure for the conversation and a simple way to present your packages, so good consults stop ending in "let me think about it".

  6. Your strategist

    Vincent on your numbers

    The founder reviews consults booked, consults shown and packages sold with you, trainer by trainer, and tells you what to change next.

Personal training marketing FAQ Straight answers.

What PT studio owners ask most before a call. Contracts, billing and the free month for any gym are answered in the gym marketing FAQ.

CoachClient1:1
Why focus on consultations instead of selling sessions online?

Personal training is a considered purchase. Most people want to talk to a trainer before they commit to a package, so a booked consultation is the realistic first step. We build the ads, the follow-up and the booking page around getting that conversation on the calendar.

Can you fill specific trainers' calendars?

Yes. Follow-up offers consult times with the trainers who have open hours, and we can feature specific trainers in ads when it makes sense for your studio.

What happens if someone misses their consultation?

Reminders go out before every consultation to help people show up. If someone misses theirs, the follow-up offers them a new time, so the lead isn't simply lost. Our gym front desk sales script covers what to say when you reach them.

Does this work for semi-private and small-group training?

Yes. Many PT studios mix one-to-one and semi-private sessions. The same system works for both: the offer and the follow-up simply point people toward the option that fits them and your open hours.
