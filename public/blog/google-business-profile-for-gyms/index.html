Google Business Profile for gyms

When someone nearby searches “gym near me”, the map results decide who gets the call. Here is how to set up and optimise your Google Business Profile, step by step, with a checklist at the end.

The short answer

To get your gym showing up for “gym near me”, claim and verify your Google Business Profile, use your real business name, choose the most specific accurate primary category, and fill in every field: hours, services, website and booking link. Add real photos regularly, ask members for reviews and reply to every one, post updates, and keep your name, address and phone number identical everywhere online. Google ranks local results on relevance, distance and prominence. You can’t move your building, but you can improve the other two.

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that appears in the map results when someone searches for a gym near them, and on Google Maps when they look around their neighbourhood. It shows your reviews, photos, hours and a button to call or visit your site, before anyone reaches your website. For a lot of gyms it is quietly the biggest source of new enquiries. For many others it is a half-finished listing someone claimed years ago and never touched.

It is free, it takes an afternoon to get right, and it keeps working while you sleep. Here is how to do it properly.

Why does a Google Business Profile matter for gyms?

People choose a gym close to home or work, so they search locally: “gym near me”, “crossfit [town]”, “personal trainer near me”, “24 hour gym”. For searches like these, Google usually shows a map with a handful of local businesses above the regular results. Those map listings come from Business Profiles, not websites.

Google says local results are based mainly on three things: relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance (how far you are from the searcher) and prominence (how well known and well reviewed your business is). You can’t change distance. Everything in this guide improves relevance and prominence. And even when you do show up, the profile is often where the decision is made: a searcher compares three gyms by their photos, star ratings and review text, and calls one of them.

Step 1: Claim and verify your profile

Search for your gym’s name on Google. If a profile already exists, whether created by you, a previous owner or Google itself, use the option to claim or manage it. If nothing exists, create one at Google’s Business Profile site. Use a Google account that belongs to the business, not a coach’s personal email, and add yourself as the owner so you never lose access when staff change.

Google will then ask you to verify that you run the business. The method varies: it may be a phone call or text, an email, a video recording showing your location and signage, or a postcard to your address. Google chooses which options it offers. Your profile won’t show fully in search until verification is complete, so do it straight away.

Step 2: Get your name, address and categories right

Business name. Use your real name, exactly as it appears on your sign. Don’t add keywords or locations (“Iron Gym | Best Gym & Personal Training Downtown”). It breaks Google’s guidelines and is a common reason profiles get suspended.

Address. Enter your actual street address, formatted the same way it appears everywhere else. A gym serves customers at its location, so your address should be visible. Don’t use a virtual office or a mailbox.

Primary category. This is one of the strongest relevance signals you control. Pick the most specific category that is genuinely true of your business. Start typing in the category field and look at the options: there are more fitness-related categories than most people expect, including general ones like “Gym” and “Fitness center” and more specific ones for particular kinds of training. Choose the one that matches how most of your members would describe you.

Secondary categories. Add a few more that describe real services you offer, such as personal training. Only add categories that are accurate. Adding every loosely related category dilutes your relevance rather than helping it.

Step 3: Fill in hours, services and links

Every empty field is a missed chance to match a search or answer a question. Fill in all of them:

  • Hours. Accurate opening hours, plus special hours for holidays. If you are open 24 hours, say so. It is often exactly what people are searching for. Update them before every holiday so nobody turns up to a locked door.
  • Description. A few plain sentences about who you are, what you offer and who it’s for. Write it for people, not for keywords.
  • Services. List what you actually sell: group classes, personal training, open gym, an intro session, nutrition coaching. Add a short description for each.
  • Attributes. Tick every attribute that applies, such as accessibility features and amenities.
  • Phone number. One that is answered, or at least followed up quickly. A missed call from your profile is a lead walking to the next listing.
  • Website and booking link. Link to the page that books an intro, not a generic homepage. If you can, add tracking parameters to the link so you can see which enquiries came from your profile.

Where that link lands matters as much as the listing. A profile that sends visitors to a slow page with no clear next step wastes the click, which is why our gym website design work starts with the page people land on from Google.

Step 4: Add real photos and video

Photos are how people decide whether a gym feels like somewhere they could belong. Upload real photos and keep adding new ones regularly, so the profile always looks current. A good starting list:

  • A clear shot of the outside and the entrance, so people recognise it when they arrive.
  • The main training floor, well lit and tidy.
  • A class in progress, with members who are happy to be in it.
  • Your coaches, individually and together.
  • Changing rooms, showers, parking, anything a nervous first-timer might wonder about.
  • A short video tour, or a few seconds of a class.
  • Your logo and a cover photo that represents the gym at its best.

Skip stock images. They tell the searcher nothing and they notice.

Step 5: Get reviews and reply to every one

Reviews feed prominence, and they are what searchers read most closely. A gym with a steady flow of recent, detailed reviews looks alive. The most reliable way to get them is to ask, personally, at the right moment: when a member hits a milestone, finishes a challenge or tells you how much they love the place. Then send them a direct link, so it takes a minute. Put the link in a text, not just on a sign.

After a milestoneText · review ask

Huge session today, [first name]. [Milestone] is a big deal! Would you mind leaving us a quick Google review? It really helps other people find us: [review link]. Thank you!

Two rules. Don’t offer anything in exchange for reviews, such as discounts or free sessions, because Google’s policies prohibit incentivised reviews. And don’t only ask your happiest members to review you while steering others away. Ask everyone at the right moment.

If remembering to ask is the hard part, automate it. For gyms we work with, we can set up a review request that texts new members your direct review link at a set point, so everyone gets asked the same way and nobody has to remember.

Reply to every review, good and bad. Thank positive reviewers by name and mention something specific. For negative ones, stay calm, acknowledge the issue, and offer to sort it out offline. Future members read your replies as closely as the reviews themselves.

Step 6: Use posts and answer questions

Posts let you publish updates, offers and events directly on your profile: a new class time, a challenge starting next month, a charity workout, a member milestone. Posting regularly keeps the profile looking active and gives searchers a reason to get in touch now. Use a real photo and a clear button, such as “Book”, pointing to the right page.

Questions. Google has changed how questions and answers appear on Business Profiles over time, and the exact feature you see may differ. Whatever form it takes, make sure the answers to common questions (do you need experience, what does it cost roughly, is there parking, can I try a class) appear on your profile in your description, services or posts, and on your website. If people can still post public questions on your listing, check them often and answer promptly, because anyone can answer them otherwise.

Step 7: Keep your name, address and phone consistent everywhere

NAP stands for name, address and phone number. Search engines cross-check your details across your website, your profile, social pages, directories and review sites. When they match exactly, Google can be more confident it has the right information about your business. When they conflict (an old phone number here, a former suite number there), it muddies the picture and confuses customers.

Write down your details in one exact format, then check your website footer and contact page, Facebook and Instagram, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Yelp and any fitness directories or booking platforms you’re listed on. Fix anything that doesn’t match, and remove or merge duplicate listings. This is slow, unglamorous work, and it is a core part of local SEO for gyms.

How your profile compounds with Google Ads

Your Business Profile and paid search do different jobs, and they strengthen each other. The profile earns free visibility in the map results over time. Google Ads puts you at the top of the results today for high-intent searches, whatever your map ranking.

They connect in practice, too. You can link your Business Profile to your Google Ads account so your ads can show your address and distance through location assets, and they can also appear on Google Maps. A searcher who sees your ad, then your map listing with a strong rating and recent photos, meets you twice before they decide, and the second meeting is the one that builds trust. Reviews and photos make your ads more convincing. Ads bring more people to your listing. That is why we tune the profile whenever we run Google Ads for gyms.

Mistakes that hurt or suspend gym profiles

  • Keyword-stuffed business names. Use your real name only.
  • Fake or virtual addresses. The address must be where you actually operate.
  • Duplicate listings. One location, one profile. Merge or remove extras.
  • Incentivised or fake reviews. Against Google’s policies and easy to spot.
  • Letting the profile go stale. Old hours, old photos and no replies suggest a gym that might not be open any more.
  • Losing access. A profile owned by a former employee’s email is a problem waiting to happen. Keep ownership with the business.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Google Business Profile free for gyms?

Yes. Creating and managing a Google Business Profile is free. You only pay if you choose to run Google Ads alongside it.

How do I get my gym to show up for “gym near me”?

Claim and verify your profile, choose an accurate primary category, fill in every field, add real photos, collect reviews steadily and reply to them, and keep your name, address and phone number consistent across the web. Distance to the searcher also matters, which you can’t change, so focus on relevance and prominence.

What category should a gym use on Google?

Use the most specific category that is genuinely true of your business as your primary category, then add a few accurate secondary categories for other real services such as personal training. Type in the category field to see what is available.

Is Google My Business the same as Google Business Profile?

Yes. Google My Business was renamed Google Business Profile. It is the same free listing that appears in Google Search and on Google Maps.

How long does it take to rank higher on Google Maps?

Fixing categories and completing your profile can help fairly quickly once Google processes the changes. Reviews and consistency build over months. Nobody can promise a specific position, because rankings depend on your competition and on where each searcher is standing.
